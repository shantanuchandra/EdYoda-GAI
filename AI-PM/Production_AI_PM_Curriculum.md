# Production AI PM

## Lead GenAI and Agentic Products from Strategy to Launch

**Version:** v1  
**Date:** 2026-06-22  
**Audience:** Senior PMs targeting global or remote AI PM roles, and senior PMs who need to lead GenAI or agentic product initiatives with credible technical judgment.  
**Platform context:** Course for Shantanu Chandra's own learning platform. EdYoda is historical context only.

---

## 1. Positioning

**Production AI PM** is a premium, practitioner-led program for senior PMs who want to become credible AI product leaders.

It is not a prompt engineering course, a coding bootcamp, or a generic AI awareness program. It teaches senior PMs how to decide which AI products are worth building, design GenAI and agentic systems with engineering teams, evaluate quality, control cost and risk, and communicate the product story at executive and interview level.

**One-line promise**

> Become the PM who can lead GenAI and agentic products from strategy to production: opportunity, architecture, evals, cost, risk, launch, and executive narrative.

**Category**

Premium cohort-based AI product leadership program.

**Core differentiation**

Most AI PM courses are either strategy-heavy, tool-heavy, or evals-only. This program combines all three into a senior PM operating system:

1. Product strategy and business judgment.
2. Production-grade GenAI and agentic system design.
3. Evals, traces, reliability, cost, and launch readiness.
4. Portfolio evidence that can support senior AI PM interviews.

---

## 2. Learner Profile

### Primary learner

Senior PMs, group PMs, lead PMs, and product leaders who already understand product discovery, roadmaps, stakeholder management, and business tradeoffs, but need AI-specific judgment and technical fluency.

### Secondary learner

Strategy leaders, operators, and product-minded business leaders who own AI product initiatives and need to work credibly with technical teams.

### Not optimized for

- Entry-level PMs who need PM fundamentals.
- Engineers who want a deep ML engineering course.
- Learners looking for only prompt engineering tricks.
- People seeking heavy ML math, statistics, or model training.
- Learners who want a pure coding bootcamp.

---

## 3. Outcome Promise

By the end, learners should be able to:

1. Identify AI product opportunities worth pursuing and kill weak AI ideas early.
2. Translate a senior business problem into a GenAI or agentic product strategy.
3. Explain a GenAI system design to engineering without pretending to be an engineer.
4. Decide when to use prompting, RAG, workflow automation, agents, APIs, fine-tuning, or non-AI logic.
5. Write an AI PRD that includes system behavior, failure modes, eval criteria, data needs, and human-in-the-loop decisions.
6. Build or direct a low-code prototype that demonstrates the product workflow.
7. Design an eval plan using golden datasets, rubrics, trace review, and regression checks.
8. Reason about cost, latency, observability, safety, security, and launch readiness.
9. Work with AI/ML engineering teams through architecture reviews and tradeoff discussions.
10. Present a senior-level AI PM portfolio and interview narrative.

---

## 4. Final Portfolio Packet

The program should not end with a certificate alone. It should end with a **Senior AI PM Portfolio Packet**.

Each learner produces:

1. **AI Product Strategy Memo**  
   Problem, users, business case, why AI, why now, risks, alternatives, and roadmap.

2. **AI PRD**  
   User journeys, system behavior, edge cases, failure modes, data sources, model assumptions, acceptance criteria, and human controls.

3. **Agent/RAG Workflow Prototype**  
   A no-code or low-code demonstration using the standard course stack.

4. **Eval Plan and Golden Dataset**  
   Test cases, expected behavior, failure categories, rubric, trace review format, and regression plan.

5. **Technical Architecture Brief for PMs**  
   Plain-English system map: model, tools, context, retrieval, memory, permissions, telemetry, and escalation paths.

6. **Production Readiness Review**  
   Cost, latency, security, safety, observability, governance, and launch gates.

7. **Executive Narrative and Interview Story**  
   A concise story explaining how the learner would lead the product with engineering, design, data, legal, GTM, and leadership.

## 5. Recommended Format

### Duration

**8 weeks** is the recommended format.

Rationale: Six weeks is possible, but too compressed for senior PMs to produce meaningful portfolio artifacts while balancing work. Eight weeks allows a premium experience without becoming a long executive program.

### Weekly rhythm

Each week:

- **Live Strategy + Studio Session:** 120 minutes, blending concept teaching, applied walkthroughs, critique, and peer discussion.
- **Assignment:** 2 to 4 hours.
- **Optional Advanced Lab:** 60 to 90 minutes, async or live.

### Cohort size

Recommended: **12 to 18 learners**.

Rationale: Small enough for meaningful critique, large enough for peer learning and discussion across domains.

### Feedback model

Premium, but not unscalable:

- Week 2: Strategy memo review.
- Week 4: AI PRD and workflow review.
- Week 5: Eval plan review.
- Week 7: Production readiness review.
- Week 8: Final portfolio review and AI PM interview narrative critique.

Optional premium add-ons:

- 1:1 capstone review.
- Mock AI PM interview.
- Resume/LinkedIn/portfolio positioning review.

## 6. Teaching Case Strategy

Use both a shared course case and personal/company context.

### Shared case

A B2B SaaS or enterprise workflow case should be used throughout live teaching. Suggested case:

**Customer Success Copilot for B2B SaaS**

Why this works:

- Familiar to senior PMs.
- Supports RAG, workflows, agents, approvals, cost, evals, trust, and launch.
- Has measurable outcomes: resolution time, expansion signals, customer risk, support quality.
- Naturally creates security and governance questions: account access, tenant isolation, sensitive data, incorrect recommendations.

### Learner capstone context

Each learner adapts the frameworks to either:

- Their current product/company problem, privacy-safe.
- A selected public domain.
- The shared case if they cannot use a real work context.

### Product examples

Use these openly as recurring examples:

- **Wasabi Travels:** AI travel experience, workflow orchestration, consumer UX, uncertainty handling.
- **Card Compass:** recommendation and decision support, ranking quality, trust, evaluation, explainability.
- **Valik Diary:** memory, personalization, context, content, knowledge workflows.

Use these only privately or internally:

- Naina Hiring.

## 7. Recommended Tool Stack

The course should teach named tools deeply enough to be useful, but always through durable principles.

### Core path: no-code / low-code

Required tools:

- **Claude and ChatGPT:** product reasoning, system prompts, structured outputs, critique, PRD drafting.
- **Perplexity or equivalent research tool:** market research, competitive scans, source-backed synthesis.
- **Notion or Google Docs:** portfolio packet, PRD, review artifacts.
- **Google Sheets or Airtable:** golden dataset, eval table, cost model, scoring rubric.
- **n8n:** workflows, tool calls, approvals, multi-step automations, agent demos.
- **Lovable, v0, or Bolt:** lightweight UI prototype for product surface.
- **Figma or FigJam:** flow maps, autonomy map, architecture diagram, human approval paths.

Recommended eval demo:

- Start with spreadsheet-based evals so every PM understands the mechanics.
- Then demo one production-style eval/tracing tool such as LangSmith, Braintrust, Humanloop, or Langfuse.

### Optional advanced path

For learners who want deeper technical credibility:

- OpenAI or Anthropic APIs.
- Claude Code, Codex, or Cursor.
- Basic GitHub portfolio.
- Simple RAG using a hosted vector store or framework.
- LangSmith, Braintrust, Humanloop, or Langfuse for traces and evals.
- PostHog, Amplitude, or warehouse-style thinking for product telemetry.

### Tool principle

Tools will change. The durable curriculum is:

- How to model workflows.
- How to design context.
- How to specify tools.
- How to evaluate outputs.
- How to reason about quality, cost, latency, risk, and user trust.

## 8. Curriculum Overview

### Week 1: AI Product Judgment for Senior PMs

**Core question:** What makes an AI product worth building, and how is AI product management different from normal product management?

**Topics**

1. Deterministic vs probabilistic products.
2. AI as capability, not feature dressing.
3. When not to use AI.
4. Automation vs augmentation.
5. Senior PM judgment in ambiguous AI spaces.
6. The Production AI PM operating model.

**120-minute blended session**

- Strategy: teardown why many AI features fail after the demo.
- Strategy: introduce the AI opportunity filter: pain, data, workflow, quality tolerance, economics, risk.
- Studio / critique: learners analyze 3 product ideas and decide which should be AI, which should be workflow automation, and which should remain non-AI.

**Assignment**

- Pick a capstone problem.
- Complete the AI Opportunity Filter.

**Portfolio artifact**

- AI Opportunity One-Pager.

**Optional advanced lab**

- Compare the same product idea using ChatGPT, Claude, and Perplexity. Identify where each tool helps and misleads.

---

### Week 2: AI Strategy, Moats, and Business Case

**Core question:** How does a senior PM make an AI product strategically defensible?

**Topics**

1. AI product strategy: user pain, workflow pain, business leverage.
2. Data advantage, distribution advantage, workflow advantage.
3. Avoiding thin API-wrapper products.
4. Build, buy, partner, or wait.
5. Executive alignment and roadmap sequencing.
6. Business case: value, cost, adoption, risk.

**120-minute blended session**

- Strategy: map AI opportunities to business outcomes and user workflow depth.
- Studio / critique: review strategy memo examples using the shared B2B SaaS case.
- Studio / critique: pressure-test learner opportunity choices against strategy, economics, and data advantage.

**Assignment**

- Draft the AI Product Strategy Memo.

**Portfolio artifact**

- AI Product Strategy Memo v1.

**Optional advanced lab**

- Use market research tools to benchmark competitor AI claims and separate real capability from positioning.

---

### Week 3: GenAI System Design for PMs

**Core question:** What does a PM need to understand about prompts, context, RAG, tools, models, and memory to work credibly with engineering?

**Topics**

1. The GenAI system map: user input, instructions, model, context, retrieval, tools, output, telemetry.
2. Prompting as product specification.
3. Context engineering: what the model needs at the moment of work.
4. RAG in PM language: chunking, retrieval, freshness, citations, permissions.
5. Tool use and APIs: what the model can read, decide, and do.
6. Choosing between prompt, RAG, fine-tuning, workflow, agent, and rules.

**120-minute blended session**

- Strategy: walk through a GenAI architecture map without code.
- Studio / critique: convert a fuzzy feature into a system behavior spec.
- Studio / critique: identify where prompts, context, retrieval, tools, and telemetry belong in learner capstones.

**Assignment**

- Draft the AI PRD sections for behavior, context, data, and constraints.

**Portfolio artifact**

- AI PRD v1.

**Optional advanced lab**

- Build a simple retrieval demo using a hosted tool or API-based example.

---

### Week 4: Agentic Product Design

**Core question:** When should a product use an agent, and how do PMs design safe autonomy?

**Topics**

1. Workflows vs agents.
2. Autonomy levels: suggest, draft, prepare, execute with approval, execute autonomously.
3. Agent tools: schemas, permissions, examples, boundaries.
4. Human-in-the-loop checkpoints.
5. Recovery paths, stop conditions, loop budgets, tool budgets.
6. Agent UX: planning visibility, approvals, undo, escalation.

**120-minute blended session**

- Strategy: agent design patterns: chaining, routing, parallelization, orchestrator-worker, evaluator-optimizer.
- Studio / critique: build an autonomy map for the shared case.
- Studio / critique: review where learner capstones need approval gates, recovery paths, and stop conditions.

**Assignment**

- Build or sketch a low-code workflow prototype in n8n or equivalent.

**Portfolio artifact**

- Agent/RAG Workflow Prototype v1.
- Autonomy and Approval Map.

**Optional advanced lab**

- Implement one tool call or API action with guardrails.

---

### Week 5: Evals as the New PRD

**Core question:** How does a PM define, measure, and improve AI quality?

**Topics**

1. Why AI quality cannot rely on happy-path demos.
2. Error analysis from real traces.
3. Golden datasets and failure mode taxonomy.
4. Rubrics, assertions, human review, and LLM-as-judge.
5. Retrieval evals vs generation evals.
6. Evals as release gates and roadmap inputs.

**120-minute blended session**

- Strategy: build an eval plan from a PRD.
- Studio / critique: review sample traces and classify failures.
- Studio / critique: turn learner acceptance criteria into golden dataset rows and rubrics.

**Assignment**

- Create a golden dataset and rubric for the learner capstone.

**Portfolio artifact**

- Eval Dataset v1.
- Eval Rubric.
- Failure Mode Taxonomy.

**Optional advanced lab**

- Run a small eval in LangSmith, Braintrust, Humanloop, Langfuse, or equivalent.

---

### Week 6: Working with AI/ML Engineering Teams

**Core question:** How does a senior PM become technically credible without becoming the engineer?

**Topics**

1. How to run an AI architecture review as a PM.
2. Model and vendor selection: capability, latency, cost, privacy, lock-in.
3. Technical tradeoffs: RAG vs fine-tuning, small vs large models, workflow vs agent.
4. Data contracts, evaluation contracts, and telemetry contracts.
5. How to read traces, dashboards, incident reports, and experiment results.
6. How to ask engineering the right questions.

**120-minute blended session**

- Strategy: architecture review simulation.
- Studio / critique: learners defend their technical architecture brief.
- Studio / critique: practice asking engineering-facing questions about model, retrieval, tools, telemetry, and failure modes.

**Assignment**

- Write the Technical Architecture Brief for PMs.

**Portfolio artifact**

- Technical Architecture Brief.

**Optional advanced lab**

- Read API docs, compare model pricing and latency, and produce a recommendation memo.

---

### Week 7: Production Readiness: Cost, Latency, Safety, and Launch

**Core question:** What must be true before this AI product should ship?

**Topics**

1. Cost per workflow, cost per user, and cost per successful outcome.
2. Latency and UX: first token, streaming, background work, progress states.
3. Observability: traces, logs, eval regressions, incidents.
4. AI security: prompt injection, sensitive data, excessive agency, vector weaknesses.
5. Governance: privacy, compliance, model risk, human escalation.
6. Launch gates: beta, staged rollout, rollback, monitoring, post-launch learning loop.

**120-minute blended session**

- Strategy: production readiness review template.
- Studio / critique: red-team the shared case and learner capstones.
- Studio / critique: identify concrete launch gates for cost, latency, safety, permissions, and fallback paths.

**Assignment**

- Complete the Production Readiness Review.

**Portfolio artifact**

- Cost and Latency Model.
- Risk Register.
- Launch Readiness Review.

**Optional advanced lab**

- Create a simple trace dashboard or event taxonomy for the product.

---

### Week 8: Executive Narrative, Portfolio, and AI PM Interview Readiness

**Core question:** How does the learner turn the work into a credible senior AI PM signal?

**Topics**

1. The senior AI PM story: strategy, system, evals, launch.
2. Board memo structure.
3. Interview narratives for global AI PM roles.
4. Explaining tradeoffs to technical and executive audiences.
5. Portfolio polish: what to show, what to hide, what to anonymize.
6. Next 90 days: how to apply this at work or in job search.

**120-minute blended session**

- Strategy: how to present AI product leadership without sounding tool-obsessed.
- Studio / critique: final portfolio critique and interview narrative review.
- Studio / critique: rehearse the executive version and the interview version of the capstone story.

**Assignment**

- Submit final portfolio packet.

**Portfolio artifact**

- Final Senior AI PM Portfolio Packet.
- AI PM Interview Narrative.

**Optional advanced lab**

- Build a public portfolio page or private case-study deck.

## 9. Capstone Options

Learners choose one of three capstone tracks.

### Track A: Enterprise Copilot

Best for B2B SaaS PMs.

Examples:

- Customer Success Copilot.
- Sales Enablement Copilot.
- Support Resolution Agent.
- Internal Knowledge Assistant.

### Track B: Agentic Workflow Product

Best for PMs interested in automation, operations, and AI agents.

Examples:

- Refund approval agent.
- Candidate screening assistant.
- Vendor onboarding workflow.
- Research and synthesis agent.

### Track C: Decision Support Product

Best for PMs interested in recommendations, ranking, personalization, and trust.

Examples:

- Financial product recommendation assistant.
- Travel planning assistant.
- Career path advisor.
- Knowledge personalization system.

Recommended default: **Track A** for the shared case, while allowing learners to pick any track for their personal capstone.

## 10. Assessment Rubric

The course should evaluate learners like senior PMs, not students memorizing concepts.

### Strategy quality

- Is the problem worth solving?
- Is AI actually necessary?
- Is the business case credible?
- Are alternatives considered?
- Is the roadmap sequenced well?

### System judgment

- Does the learner understand the major system components?
- Are context, tools, retrieval, and memory specified clearly?
- Are model and vendor assumptions reasonable?
- Are failure modes explicit?

### Evaluation quality

- Does the eval set reflect real user and business risk?
- Are rubrics specific enough?
- Are failure categories actionable?
- Is there a regression plan?
- Are evals connected to launch decisions?

### Production readiness

- Are cost and latency addressed concretely?
- Are privacy, permissions, and security risks handled?
- Are human approvals and fallback paths clear?
- Is there a staged launch plan?

### Executive communication

- Can the learner explain the product to leadership?
- Can they work credibly with engineering?
- Can they defend tradeoffs?
- Can the portfolio support a senior AI PM interview?

## 11. What This Course Should Avoid

Avoid:

- Heavy ML math.
- Generic PM basics.
- Coding bootcamp positioning.
- Prompt-engineering-only framing.
- Tool-chasing without product judgment.
- Demo-first teaching without evals or production readiness.
- Compliance theater without practical product gates.
- Claims that learners will become ML engineers.

Do:

- Teach technical fluency without pretending PMs need to become engineers.
- Teach named tools, but anchor them in durable principles.
- Use real product examples.
- Make every module contribute to the final portfolio.
- Make evals, traces, and launch gates central.
